About kinds of day and patient mixes

Kinds of day

Patient mix

The kind of day sets the situation, like Kim on the desk with a 3 PM rush. The patient mix picks the particular crowd: who walks in and when, and how long each visit takes.

The same number always plays out exactly the same afternoon, so a problem can be found again and re-checked after a fix. “Try a different crowd” picks a new number. The tests run 20 patient mixes of every kind of day.

What this is for

A prototype of a product a community clinic could run, tested before any real patient sees it.

For patients

Know roughly how long the wait is before coming in or while waiting, by web page or text, in English or Spanish.

For front desk staff

Built to cut down on “how long?” questions. One tap when a visit runs long; approve or reject suggestions with a one-line reason.

For clinic leaders

Built to keep people from leaving before they’re seen, at a modeled cost of cents a day, with every number traced to the record.

What this demo shows, and what it can’t

It shows how the product behaves when the people feeding it are imperfect: staff who fall behind, patients who arrive late or text about something urgent.

  • No real clinic, staff member or patient has used it yet.
  • Every person and visit is synthetic; costs and staff time are modeled.
  • No triage or medical advice anywhere.
  • Read how it’s built on GitHub

What these numbers can’t tell you

This is a simulated clinic with synthetic people. Visit lengths and arrivals follow published averages, not one clinic’s records.

  • No front-desk worker, clinician or patient has used it yet.
  • Staff time and costs are modeled, not measured.
  • Not connected to a real scheduling system; no real texts are sent.
  • No compliance or privacy review has been done.
